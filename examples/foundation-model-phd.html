Before the formalism

What a foundation model is

A single large neural network, pretrained with self-supervision on broad, mostly unlabeled data, then adapted (fine-tuned or prompted) to many downstream tasks.

The key idea: replace many task-specific models, each trained on its own labeled dataset, with one general model trained once and reused. The term was coined at Stanford in 2021 to name a shift that BERT and GPT-3 had already started.

Why the field cares
  1. Leverage: one artifact serves hundreds of applications.
  2. Predictability: loss improves smoothly with scale, so investment can be planned (slide 5).
  3. Surprise: behaviors nobody trained for, like learning a task from a few examples in the prompt, appear at scale (slides 7–8).

Problem statement & formal setup

Definition

Two-stage learning

A model "trained on broad data at scale" that is "adaptable to a wide range of downstream tasks." Formally:

$$\theta^\star=\arg\min_\theta\;\mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mathcal{D}_{\text{pre}}}\big[\ell_{\text{SSL}}(x;\theta)\big]$$ $$\theta_{\mathcal{T}}=\mathcal{A}\big(\theta^\star,\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}\big),\qquad |\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{T}}|\ll|\mathcal{D}_{\text{pre}}|$$

\(\mathcal{A}\) ranges from full fine-tuning to the identity map with a prompt (in-context learning). Bommasani et al. single out two consequences: emergence (behavior is induced, not specified) and homogenization (many systems share one \(\theta^\star\), inheriting its defects).

Canonical self-supervised objectives \(\ell_{\text{SSL}}\)
Autoregressive
GPT · predict next
\(-\sum_t \log p_\theta(x_t\mid x_{<t})\)
Masked LM
BERT · fill the blanks
\(-\sum_{i\in\mathcal{M}} \log p_\theta(x_i\mid x_{\setminus\mathcal{M}})\)
Contrastive
CLIP · match pairs (diagonal)
\(-\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i \log \frac{\exp(\langle u_i,v_i\rangle/\tau)}{\sum_j \exp(\langle u_i,v_j\rangle/\tau)}\)
symmetrized over image→text and text→image
Sources: Bommasani et al. 2021 · 2108.07258 · Brown et al. 2020 · 2005.14165 · Devlin et al. 2019 · 1810.04805 · Radford et al. 2021 · 2103.00020

Core method · architecture & compute

Substrate

Transformers make scale tractable

$$\mathrm{Attn}(Q,K,V)=\mathrm{softmax}\!\Big(\tfrac{QK^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}+M\Big)V$$

\(M\) is the causal mask (\(-\infty\) above the diagonal) for autoregressive models. Per layer, attention costs \(O(n^2 d)\) in sequence length \(n\), but all positions train in parallel, unlike recurrent models, which is the property that made \(10^{23}\)-FLOP runs practical.

Causal mask \(M\): token \(i\) attends only to positions \(j \le i\) (filled). The empty upper triangle is set to \(-\infty\) before the softmax, so a model trained on next-token prediction can never peek at the answer.

Training compute, to first order
$$C\;\approx\;6\,N\,D\ \ \text{FLOPs}$$

\(N\) non-embedding parameters, \(D\) training tokens; the factor 6 counts the forward (2N) and backward (4N) pass per token.

Sanity check, GPT-3: \(N = 1.75\times10^{11}\), \(D \approx 3\times10^{11}\) tokens
\(\Rightarrow C \approx 6 \cdot 1.75\times10^{11}\cdot 3\times10^{11} \approx 3.2\times10^{23}\) FLOPs,
matching the 3.14×10²³ reported by Brown et al.
Sources: Vaswani et al., NeurIPS 2017 · 1706.03762 · Kaplan et al. 2020, §2.1 · 2001.08361 · Brown et al. 2020 · 2005.14165

Empirical power laws, and a disagreement about allocation

Kaplan (2020) vs Chinchilla (2022)

Kaplan et al. · single-variable fits
$$L(X)=\Big(\tfrac{X_c}{X}\Big)^{\alpha_X}$$
\(\alpha_N\approx0.076,\ N_c\approx8.8\times10^{13}\) · \(\alpha_D\approx0.095,\ D_c\approx5.4\times10^{13}\) · \(\alpha_C^{\min}\approx0.050\)
Compute-optimal: \(N_{\text{opt}}\propto C^{0.73}\). Prescription: grow the model much faster than the data.
Hoffmann et al. · joint parametric fit
$$L(N,D)=E+\frac{A}{N^{\alpha}}+\frac{B}{D^{\beta}}$$
\(E=1.69,\ A=406.4,\ B=410.7,\ \alpha=0.34,\ \beta=0.28\)
Compute-optimal: \(N_{\text{opt}}\propto C^{a},\ D_{\text{opt}}\propto C^{b}\), \(a\approx b\approx0.5\) (three methods: 0.50/0.49/0.46). Scale both equally.
Evidence · read with (b)

At the same budget (~5.76×10²³ FLOPs, dashed line), Chinchilla (70B params, 1.4T tokens) outperforms Gopher (280B, ~300B tokens). Each minimum in (a) is one point on the Hoffmann line in (b). The gap from Kaplan is attributed largely to its fixed learning-rate schedule and token count across runs.

From full fine-tuning to no gradients at all

Adaptation, ordered by parameters touched

Full fine-tuning
\(\theta\leftarrow\theta-\eta\nabla_\theta\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}}\) over all of \(\theta\)
BERT: SOTA on 11 NLP tasks with one added output layer
LoRA
\(W=W_0+BA,\ B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r},A\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times k},\ r\ll\min(d,k)\)
Freezes \(W_0\); on GPT-3 cuts trainable params ~10,000× and GPU memory ~3×
RLHF
\(\max_{\pi_\theta}\ \mathbb{E}\big[r_\phi(x,y)\big]-\beta\,\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi_\theta\,\|\,\pi_{\text{ref}}\big)\)
InstructGPT: 1.3B outputs preferred by labelers over 175B GPT-3
In-context learning
\(y\sim p_{\theta^\star}(\cdot\mid \text{demos}_{1:k},\,x)\); \(\theta\) unchanged
GPT-3: few-shot gains grow with model size

Is emergence real?

Phase transition or measurement artifact

Wei et al. 2022
An ability is emergent if it is "not present in smaller models but is present in larger models"; documents near-chance performance followed by sharp jumps on many BIG-bench and multi-step reasoning tasks.
Schaeffer et al. 2023
Argues the jumps come from the researcher's metric: nonlinear or discontinuous scores (exact match) turn smooth per-token gains into apparent cliffs; continuous metrics (token edit distance, Brier) give predictable curves.

Status: unresolved. Per-token loss scales smoothly; whether task-level capabilities can be forecast from it is the practical question for safety and planning.

Sources: Wei et al., "Emergent Abilities of Large Language Models," TMLR 2022 · 2206.07682 · Schaeffer et al., "Are Emergent Abilities of LLMs a Mirage?," NeurIPS 2023 · 2304.15004

Where the paradigm is under strain

Open problems

Data constraints
Compute-optimal scaling consumes tokens linearly with \(N\). With repeated data, up to ~4 epochs is nearly as good as unique data, after which returns decay quickly; how far synthetic data can substitute is open.
Factuality
\(\ell_{\text{SSL}}\) rewards plausibility, not truth; calibration and grounding (retrieval, tools) are patches, not guarantees.
Evaluation
Benchmark contamination from web-scale pretraining sets, saturation, and metric choice (see slide 8) blur what is measured; holistic suites like HELM respond to this.
Homogenization
Shared \(\theta^\star\) creates correlated failure modes and concentrates power among the few actors who can afford pretraining.
Meaning & understanding
Contested. Bender & Koller argue a system trained only on form cannot in principle learn meaning; others point to world-model probes. No consensus.
